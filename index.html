Welcome to 70-467 Machine Learning for Business Analytics!

In an era when AI can write code faster than most people, writing code is not the scarce skill. The bottleneck in productive analytics is understanding, verification, and judgment: knowing what to ask the tools to do, checking whether the answer is good enough to act on, and explaining both the recommendation and its limits. AI will not replace your job. Someone who can use it and is prepared to work those bottlenecks will.

This course prepares you for that. You cannot evaluate the tools, or incorporate them into how you work, until you understand the methods. That is what sharpens judgment and makes the communication defensible. We incentivize responsible use of AI: tools that augment you, not substitutes that replace the thinking. You will learn to turn data into decisions you can stand behind. Choose the method, check it carefully, and explain the recommendation and its limits. The emphasis is business judgment under real problems, not model fitting as an end in itself. We work in R, with real datasets.

How we work

A simple weekly rhythm so you always know how to prepare.

01 · Before class

Lecture notes

Read the assigned textbook sections and, when assigned, run the Quarto notes. Those notes are in Quarto, so complete the setup instructions early. You need a working machine for the week to week flow of the course.

02 · Monday

Concepts & decisions

Mondays are for understanding the method, always starting from a business problem. You prepare a short introduction to that method and present it. I then take over and finish the lecture. The presentation schedule comes in week two, once enrollment is set.

03 · Wednesday

R lab

Wednesdays are labs, and we start from a problem, not from the code. The problem is posted before class. Come prepared. In class we work through guided notebooks: some of the code is already there, you complete the TODOs, we run it, and we interpret the output together. Then we argue the merits of the analysis and how to make it stronger.

04 · Outside class

Build and defend

For homework you get real data, a problem, and starter code. You turn in a notebook, at most six slides, and a video of at most five minutes — same story in all three. The project is individual and ends with an in-class presentation; details are in the syllabus.
